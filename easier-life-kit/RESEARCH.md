# Evidence and editorial notes

Researched 10 October 2026. The dialogue is original. This is public education, not a diagnostic tool. Practical examples and analogies are the author's suggestions, not interventions tested exactly as written. No claim can establish a particular person's honesty or private feelings.

## Scenes 1-3: Deception

Bond, C. F., Jr., & DePaulo, B. M. (2006). Accuracy of deception judgments. Personality and Social Psychology Review, 10(3), 214-234.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16859438/

The meta-analysis synthesised 206 documents and 24,483 judges; reported average truth/lie classification accuracy was 54%. The chart uses 50% as the chance benchmark for the binary research task and 54% as the rounded pooled human average. These are not two experimental groups, personal accuracy scores, or probabilities that a particular person is lying. The display shows a zero baseline; no invented uncertainty intervals. Results from research tasks do not transfer mechanically to every real conversation.

American Psychological Association (2016), Deception detection:
https://www.apa.org/monitor/2016/03/deception

The script rejects a reliable single body-language tell. Nervousness and gaze are not proof of lying. The clarification and verification routine is practical guidance, not a validated detector. Neither consistency nor inconsistency establishes honesty. Do not use this script to justify coercive questioning, surveillance, or accusations.

## Scene 4: Feeling cared for

Does Partner Responsiveness Predict Hedonic and Eudaimonic Well-Being? A 10-Year Longitudinal Study.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28592909/

Perceived responsiveness concerns feeling cared for, appreciated and understood. The longitudinal study of more than 2,000 married US adults reported prediction of later eudaimonic wellbeing. It does not establish causation, reveal intent, or validate a friendship test for children. The script explicitly limits this inference. Reply speed is not presented as a psychological measure.

## Scene 5: Requesting support

Original practical communication examples, informed broadly by responsiveness research above. No claim that requesting support guarantees it or that missed support proves indifference. Capacity and context matter. No secret loyalty tests.

## Scene 6: Responses to good news

Gable, S. L., Gonzaga, G. C., & Strachman, A. (2006). Will you be there for me when things go right? Supportive responses to positive event disclosures.
https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Shelly-Gable/publication/6737400_Will_You_Be_There_for_Me_When_Things_Go_Right_Supportive_Responses_to_Positive_Event_Disclosures/links/0046351ace0addd9fb000000/Will-You-Be-There-for-Me-When-Things-Go-Right-Supportive-Responses-to-Positive-Event-Disclosures.pdf

Related primary study: Failure to capitalize on sharing good news with romantic partners: Exploring positivity deficits of socially anxious people with self-reports, partner-reports, and behavioral observations.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3776926/

Supportive responses to positive disclosures relate to relationship quality. Research includes adult romantic couples; applying the conversational example to friendship is a practical extension. A muted reaction does not diagnose jealousy. The social-anxiety study is particularly relevant to avoiding that inference. No causal guarantee or numerical effect is claimed.

## Scenes 7-8: Boundaries and interpretations

Original practical guidance, not scored instruments or claimed experimental protocols. Distinguishing observations from interpretations is a reflection prompt, not a reason to discount repeated mistreatment. Boundaries describe the speaker's actions and limits. Disappointment does not establish lack of care; threats or repeated harmful behaviour deserve attention regardless of motives. Safety takes precedence over confrontation.

## Scene 9: Self-distancing through language

Kross et al. (2014). Self-talk as a regulatory mechanism: how you do it matters.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24467424/
Author-hosted paper:
https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/emotion-selfcontrol-psych/wp-content/uploads/sites/1322/2024/07/KrossJ_Pers_Soc_Psychol2014Self-talk_as_a_regulatory_mechanism_How_you_do_it_matters.pdf

Experiments examined non-first-person self-talk, including first impressions and public speaking. The script offers a small exercise consistent with that work. It does not claim universal effectiveness, a cure for anxiety disorders, or replacement for support or treatment.

## Scene 10: Implementation intentions

Attaining personal goals: self-concordance plus implementation intentions equals success (2002).
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12088128/

The research reports associations and experimental findings involving implementation intentions and goal progress. If-then planning connects a situation to an intended action. Homework and angry-message examples are original applications, not separately validated protocols. Plans do not remove resource constraints, illness or other obstacles; no multiplier or universal success rate is asserted.

## Scene 11: Recap

Summarises the qualified guidance above. No additional empirical claim. The aim is better judgement and communication, not manipulation, mind reading, or labelling people as disordered.
