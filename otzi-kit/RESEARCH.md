# Ötzi research and fact-check notes

Prepared 2 October 2026. This brief supports SCRIPT.md. Museum material supplies the archaeological overview; primary papers refine appearance, food, equipment, and death. The comedy is newly written and fictional. Source keys below correspond to the script’s take notes.

## The editorial position

Ötzi was a real Copper Age person, not a generic “prehistoric caveman.” The film should make his practical skills visible and use modern bureaucracy, outdoor gear marketing, and documentary conventions as joke targets. His violent death deserves a restrained treatment. Humour comes from the imagined exchanges; factual narration identifies what the evidence can establish.

The biggest corrections to older popular accounts are his likely appearance and uncertainty over the exact sequence of his death. Avoid turning either a DNA prediction or a forensic hypothesis into a photographic certainty.

## Findings and sources

### S1 — Discovery, reporting, and the border

Erika and Helmut Simon found the body on 19 September 1991 near Tisenjoch/Giogo di Tisa, at about 3,210 metres. Their report reached the authorities through the mountain refuge. Initially, it looked like a recent mountaineering fatality. Later surveying placed the find 92.56 metres inside Italy. Recovery and recognition of the body’s antiquity unfolded over several days.

**Use:** takes 01–04 and 14. The hikers’ dialogue and police exchange are sketches, not records. A 1991 refuge setting avoids a modern smartphone scene.

[South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology: The discovery](https://www.iceman.it/en/oetzi/the-discovery)

### S2 — Age, size, and identity

The museum estimates that he died at about 45, stood approximately 1.60 metres tall, and weighed around 50 kilograms during life. These are reconstructions, not civil records. His name, profession, rank, and biography are unknown. Proposed occupations should remain possibilities. The “Otzi” speaker name is only the app’s simplified spelling.

**Use:** takes 04 and 13. Do not claim he was definitely a shaman, chief, fugitive, or shepherd.

[South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology: Who was Ötzi?](https://www.iceman.it/en/oetzi/who-was-oetzi)

### S3 — Dating and the 2023 genome

The high-coverage genome paper gives a radiocarbon range of 3350–3120 calibrated BCE. Its findings support relatively dark pigmentation and a predisposition to male-pattern baldness. Exact facial features and hairstyle remain reconstructive choices. The study also revises ancestry estimates: strong early Anatolian-farmer-related ancestry is not evidence of a modern nationality or a Turkish birthplace.

**Use:** takes 02 and 05. Our balding, brown-skinned avatar is evidence-informed illustration; beard, proportions, and expression are artistic. Genetic predispositions are not diagnoses.

[Wang et al., Cell Genomics, 2023: High-coverage genome of the Tyrolean Iceman](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10504632/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xgen.2023.100377)

### S4 — Clothing as technology

His clothing assemblage included hide garments, separate leggings, a loincloth, a fur cap, and complex shoes incorporating grass insulation. The layered construction matters more to this film than a complete garment inventory. Not every piece survives completely; some details in any clothed reconstruction must be inferred.

**Use:** take 06. The cartoon’s short sleeves and simplified stitching are design decisions. Do not present the model sheet as a museum reconstruction or add a supposedly certain grass cape.

[South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology: Clothing](https://www.iceman.it/en/oetzi/clothing)

### S5 — Which animals supplied the clothes?

Ancient DNA identified sheep and goat in the coat, goat in the leggings, sheep in the loincloth, and brown bear in the cap. The study also identified cattle in a shoelace and roe deer in the quiver. These findings concern sampled objects; they do not mean every part of the outfit was tested or perfectly reconstructed.

**Use:** take 06. The mixed-hide joke has a factual basis. Keep the cap brown and fur-like, not a horned helmet.

[O’Sullivan et al., Scientific Reports, 2016: A whole mitochondria analysis of the Tyrolean Iceman’s leather](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep31279)

### S6 — Equipment

His belongings included a copper axe, flint dagger, unfinished yew bow, arrows, and smaller implements. The copper axe is a defining object, but stone tools continued to be useful. “Copper Age” should not be substituted with Bronze Age, Iron Age, or Ice Age.

**Use:** take 07. The avatar carries a simplified copper-coloured blade on a wooden haft. No steel axe, modern backpack, metal zipper, or manufactured camping stove.

[South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology: Equipment](https://www.iceman.it/en/oetzi/equipment)

### S7 — Wear and repair

Microscopic study of the lithic toolkit examines manufacture, use, maintenance, and material sources. The objects show use and resharpening, and aspects of the kit were in poor condition. This supports a practical discussion of repair. It does not independently establish a cinematic chase or a precise route for his final hours.

**Use:** take 07. “Not all ready for action” is deliberately broader than asserting a specific confrontation.

[Wierer et al., PLOS ONE, 2018: The Iceman’s lithic toolkit](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0198292)

### S8 — The axe’s long-distance connection

Chemical and lead-isotope comparisons associate the axe’s copper with southern Tuscany. Material provenance and an individual’s travel history are different claims. Ore, copper, or finished objects could pass between people.

**Use:** take 07. The delivery joke refers to a connection across distance; it does not assert that Ötzi visited the mine, used a particular trade route, or ordered an axe himself.

[Artioli et al., PLOS ONE, 2017: Long-distance connections in the Copper Age](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0179263)

### S9 — Last meal

Analysis of stomach contents identified wild animal tissues including ibex and red deer, cereal, and substantial fat; bracken traces were also present. The paper discusses a high-energy meal and several possible explanations for the plant material. The contents are a final-meal sample, not a complete dietary history.

**Use:** take 08. Do not turn the finding into a modern diet prescription. Avoid confidently calling the bracken medicine, poison, seasoning, or wrapping: its role is uncertain.

[Maixner et al., Current Biology, 2018: The Iceman’s last meal](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6065529/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2018.05.067)

### S10 — Body evidence

The museum documents 61 tattoos, an arrowhead discovered in 2001, a hand wound, and head injury. Tattoo therapy is a hypothesis. Natural preservation differs from deliberate embalming. For the fatal sequence, read the museum overview alongside S13 rather than repeating a certain “death within minutes.”

**Use:** takes 09–12. Our frozen illustration is non-anatomical. The clothed avatar does not display an accurate map of every tattoo.

[South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology: The body](https://www.iceman.it/en/oetzi/the-body)

### S11 — Nickname and modern care

The “Iceman” is about 5,300 years old. The nickname Ötzi refers to the Ötztal Alps. The South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology in Bolzano has housed him since 1998. The remains are a real person, not simply a crime-show prop.

**Use:** takes 04 and 12. The fictional narrator Mira is not a named museum employee and should not be presented as its spokesperson.

[South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology: The Iceman](https://www.iceman.it/en/oetzi/the-iceman)

### S12 — Society and period

Copper Age communities practised farming, worked materials skilfully, and participated in exchanges. Ötzi belongs in that context. “Primitive” language can hide the sophistication visible in the equipment and clothing.

**Use:** takes 04 and 13. Do not show dinosaurs, Vikings, pyramids as his home, or an entire society living in a single cave. His ordinary life remains much less accessible than his preserved belongings.

[South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology: The Copper Age](https://www.iceman.it/en/oetzi/the-copper-age)

### S13 — The 2025 forensic qualification

A reconstruction using CT data measured an approximately 110 mL haematoma and questioned whether the visible internal blood loss by itself demonstrates immediate death. The authors could not exclude external bleeding. This qualifies the mechanism and timing; it does not erase the arrow injury or prove a nonviolent death. The abstract supports the limited claim used here; this brief does not reconstruct inaccessible details of the full paper.

**Use:** take 11. Say “debated,” not “solved.” The silhouette supplies no evidence about the attacker’s sex, clothing, appearance, motive, or number of accomplices.

[Villa et al., International Journal of Legal Medicine, 2025: Forensic 3D reconstructions](https://researchprofiles.ku.dk/en/publications/%C3%B6tzi-the-iceman-forensic-3d-reconstructions-of-a-5300-year-ago-mu/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00414-025-03510-5)

## Fact-check before publication

- Keep “about” and “roughly” in reconstructed ages, sizes, and dates.
- Label the opening and police sketch as fictional reenactments. The real discoverers were the Simons; Milo and Nova are stand-ins.
- Do not call invented Ötzi dialogue a translation or a recovered quotation.
- Do not present proposed tattoo treatment as proven acupuncture.
- Separate evidence of an arrow injury from a certain minute-by-minute death narrative.
- Avoid a fully specified murderer, feud, robbery, pursuit, or last conversation.
- Describe natural ice preservation without claiming a perfectly uninterrupted 5,300-year freeze in one position.
- Keep source links in the published description so viewers can distinguish the research from the jokes.
